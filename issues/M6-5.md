# M6-5 Renew the secretary without typing into it

## Why

A long-running secretary fills its context. paddock measured that ending its terminal and launching a fresh one from a handover works and leaves workers running.

## What to build

- `makiba renew --handover-file <f>`: the secretary's request. `makibad` stores the handover, waits until the secretary's terminal is `idle` or `done`, ends and removes it, revokes its key, launches a new secretary whose task is to resume from the stored handover, and writes `secretary_renewed`.
- Workers are not touched; messages for `secretary` stay `pending` across the gap and are delivered to the new terminal.
- If the new secretary is not verified, a notice goes to the log and `makiba status` shows it.
- When the secretary's terminal ends without a renewal (`secretary_gone`, spec section 11), `makibad` launches a new secretary by the same path, from the last stored handover or, if there is none, from the open pending entries and running tasks in `state.db`. It tries at most twice in ten minutes (first proposal); after that it stops, and `makiba status` shows the secretary as gone until the person runs `makiba up`. Nothing tells the person before stage 2: the log and `makiba status` are all there is.

## Definition of done

The first commit adds `tests/test_renew.py`, failing, with the trailer `Acceptance-Test: tests/test_renew.py`. At head it passes and asserts:

- After a renewal the terminal named `secretary` is a different terminal; every worker terminal is the same one.
- A message queued during the gap is delivered to the new secretary.
- Open pending entries and `in_use` runs are identical before and after, by id.
- A secretary terminal killed with no renewal raises `secretary_gone` once and is relaunched; messages queued while it was gone stay `pending` (none is `failed`) and are delivered to the new terminal once; after two failed relaunches in the window no third is tried and the secretary shows as gone.
